# Journey invitations and membership

**Astra-reviewed plan · 21 November 2026.** Build on the private journey already shared by web, iPhone and Android. An organiser invites companions by email. Each verified recipient explicitly accepts as a **Participant** and opens the same journey. Each active member sees it once in Journeys, in its existing schedule group, and the Dashboard includes it in the existing totals. This slice covers invitations, roster, removal and departure. Coauthor and publisher assignments, administrative transfer, duplicate journey recovery and source/export grants follow their own workflows. [Requirements TRP-02, TRP-03, TRP-04 and TRP-06](../core/product-requirements.md#4-create-or-join-a-journey)

## 1. Account onboarding and journey creation

Account onboarding ends after **Welcome** and **Get started**. It respects an incoming invitation or another explicit workspace destination. A regular sign-up lands on Dashboard with the selected first action.

The Create journey form offers **Save journey** and **Create & invite people**. Both use the same private save operation and retry the same creation attempt if its response is lost. **Create & invite people** opens the invitation composer for the saved journey. The organiser can use **Find on Kairn**, **Invite by email**, or **Skip for now**. **Done** and **Skip for now** open the saved journey. The organiser can manage invitations in People.

Existing app builds can complete required onboarding through the former companion setup API. It reports that the optional checkpoint is unavailable. The previous screen redirects into the workspace.

## 2. People and invitation screens

- **Journey detail → People.** Add a small people summary beside the existing journey overview and a dedicated People destination reachable from it. The People screen lists active members by display name and role. Members see accepted participants in the current journey. Pending addresses and delivery details are visible only to active administrators. The workspace and public reader keep their existing access rules. Display **Participant** when membership has no extra capability and label existing administrator, coauthor and publisher capabilities individually. Mark **You** in the list.
- **Send invitations.** Open an inline composer on wide web and a focused full-screen form on phones. The shared field supports one email at a time and lets the organiser add several validated addresses before Send. Show the selected journey and **Participant** with one line about access. Keep the composer open after a partial batch, summarise confirmed rows and focus the first editable failure. Retry only unsaved rows after a confirmed result. Retry the original batch key after an uncertain response. **Done** is explicit after any confirmed success and preserves successful rows if there are remaining errors. Use **Invitation queued** while the worker is handling it, **Sent** once SMTP has accepted it, and **Couldn’t send** with a retry action if delivery exhausts its attempts. Use the same response for registered and unregistered addresses and restrict already-member resolution to the caller’s own journey. Separate pending invitations from active members.
- **Manage invitations.** Pending rows show expiry and actions for **Resend** and **Cancel invitation**. Resend replaces the previous link and shows the new expiry. A confirmation for cancellation explains that the email can remain in the recipient’s inbox. When acceptance already won, refresh the roster and offer member removal through the separate confirmation. Expired or failed rows can be sent again subject to the same rules.
- **Remove or leave.** An active administrator can remove an eligible member. A member can leave from People using a confirmation that names the journey and explains immediate loss of access. If they are the last active administrator, explain that the journey needs an active administrator and block the action. Accepted transfer and the account-deletion freeze path remain separate lifecycle workflows. Use the lifecycle confirmation state while the change is awaiting independent verification. A departing member returns to Journeys and sees the refreshed list, with a pending confirmation if the local deny was committed. Source ownership and personal captures follow the existing ownership rules. [Lifecycle requirements](../core/product-requirements.md#161-withdrawal-and-ownership-changes)
- **Invitation review.** Opening the email goes to a generic interstitial for an unauthenticated visitor. Once the expected verified account is established, show the journey title, inviter’s display name, local schedule, expiry and **Participant** access. Provide **Join journey** and **Back to Dashboard**. A wrong-account view offers **Switch account** and gives no identifying details. Expired and cancelled invitations offer a path to ask the organiser for a new one. An already-joined recipient may open the journey while their membership is current. An unavailable link offers **Go to Journeys** for an authenticated person or **Sign in** for a signed-out person. Opening or previewing an email never accepts the invitation.
- **Journey history and Dashboard.** Keep the existing active, upcoming, past and incomplete schedule logic. Show **Organiser** or **Participant** where it helps identify the relationship on a journey card or detail. Dashboard’s counts derive from active memberships. Provision the data for a later companion summary only from people the current caller can see in shared journeys. Add that widget after roster visibility and removal are verified. [Existing Dashboard rules](first-run-onboarding-and-dashboard.md#dashboard-and-navigation)

Use Kairn’s Source Sans 3 typography, tokens, appearances and existing screen patterns. Preserve focus and scroll when returning to People. Use a one-column flow on narrow web and native with the keyboard open. Use labelled 44 CSS px web controls and native minimum touch targets, pronounce roles and delivery states with screen readers, express state with text and icons, and respect reduced motion. Test 320 CSS px web width at 200% text, larger iOS text and Android font scale. ## 3. Email link and account handoff

1. The email points to a first-party invitation landing route with an opaque, seven-day credential. Prefer a URL fragment to keep the credential out of the initial HTTP request. Web exchanges it in a private request for a short-lived invitation continuation, then removes it from the visible URL. A verified app link may do the same through the native API. If verified routing on a platform is unavailable, finish the whole flow in the browser. Configure the sender and destination origin server-side. Disable link tracking and third-party assets on the landing surface. Keep link credentials, recipient addresses and continuation values out of access logs, analytics and crash reports.
2. Extend the typed return allowlists to support People by validated journey ID and an invitation-review destination with no credential parameter. Keep a single invitation continuation separately from ordinary workspace navigation, in web per-tab session state or native secure storage. Bound it by both its own expiry and the invitation expiry. If the recipient opens a second invitation in the same tab or app, confirm before replacing the current continuation. Bind the continuation only after the server verifies the expected email. Keep sensitive state out of route parameters, redirects and generic query caches. **Switch account** may preserve an unbound wrong-account continuation using a dedicated handoff. Ordinary sign-out, expiry and abandonment clear it. Keep the existing email verification callback and its allowlist. When verification finishes in a different tab, browser or device, return to the original tab/app or reopen the invitation email. Keep the verification itself valid across those contexts. [Web return routing](../../apps/web/src/lib/journey-return.ts) · [Native return routing](../../apps/mobile/src/workspace-return.ts)
3. The verified recipient explicitly posts **Join journey** with a stable operation ID and keeps that ID until the response is confirmed. The service commits the invitation decision, membership and an acceptance receipt keyed to the verified account, invitation and operation ID, with the accepted membership generation. The private status/retry path checks that generation and current authorised membership before returning the canonical ID, even if the continuation no longer authorises a new join. An already-accepted approved link follows the same membership check. A removed person cannot reopen the journey through either path. On success, invalidate account-scoped history, detail, People and Dashboard queries, clear the handoff and navigate to the existing detail ID. An invitation accepted in a browser is available in the app after sign-in or refresh to the same account.

## 4. Shared API and data flow

Extend the existing Journey domain and publish typed contracts in `packages/contracts` with OpenAPI parity. Both clients use the same verified-session handling, expected-account guard, private response headers and SQL permission boundaries. Route shapes below are proposed names.

| Operation | Contract and enforcement |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /api/v1/journeys/{id}/people` and `…/invitations` | Active member only for the paged roster. Administrators use the separate paged invitation projection for pending addresses and delivery states. History and detail continue to use current membership. |
| `GET /api/v1/journeys/invitable` | Page over the caller’s active administration capabilities on non-archived journeys. The previous onboarding picker used this projection. It remains available to older callers. |
| `POST /api/v1/journeys/{id}/invitations` | Active administrator on the selected non-archived journey. Use bounded batches with an idempotency key and structured per-address results. Record accepted rows, the corresponding audit entries and email jobs transactionally. |
| `POST /api/v1/journeys/{id}/invitations/{invitationId}/resend` and `/cancel` | Scope the invitation to the journey and recheck authority. Resend creates a new revision and invalidates the old link. Cancel denies acceptance and prevents future sends. |
| `POST /api/v1/journey-invitations/continue`, `/review`, `/accept` and `/acceptance-status` | Exchange the landing credential without side effects on membership. Review and accept require a current verified session and recipient match. Successful accept and authorised receipt lookup return the canonical ID. |
| `POST /api/v1/journeys/{id}/members/{memberId}/remove` and `/leave` | Recheck current authority and the last administrator rule inside the mutation. Use a stable operation ID for retries and return local denial plus the independent confirmation status. |

**Authority and privacy.** Use current active membership and `canManageJourney` for administration. Participant is an active membership with zero extra capabilities. The shared detail and schedule follow existing membership eligibility. The review draft, editorial actions, publication controls and private originals remain governed by their own capabilities and grants. Derive recipient match from the authenticated identity’s currently verified email using the identity service’s canonical comparison. Treat addresses as distinct unless identity does. Keep the current self-scoped Journey RLS policies. Add narrowly privileged projection and write operations for roster management and the verified first join, with limited SQL grants, pinned search paths and tests using the runtime database role. [Existing access function](../../packages/backend/src/modules/journeys/permissions.ts) · [Current RLS](../../packages/backend/drizzle/0004_private_journey_operations.sql)

**State and transactions.** Store ID, journey, issuing administrator, canonical recipient, grant scope, token digest, revision, creation/expiry, decision and accepted account ID in a scoped invitation record. Keep delivery state separately. A live invitation is pending and within expiry. Other decision states are accepted, cancelled, replaced or expired. Enforce one current pending invitation per journey and recipient with a database constraint and retire stale rows inside the write transaction. Validate bounded input and apply rate limits per actor, journey and recipient, plus a mail cooldown. Make identical retries return the recorded outcome and reject a changed payload under an existing key. Keep pending email accessible only as needed for delivery and admin review. Remove expired mail payloads and retain minimal audit metadata under the existing retention policy.

**Accept into the existing row.** Use a consistent lock order for accept, resend, cancel, removal and future role changes. Recheck invitation revision, expiry, inviter’s active authority, account verification, current journey status and requested scope in the transaction. End an inviter’s outstanding invitations when their membership or administrator capability is removed, so regaining authority does not reopen issued links. The existing `(journey_id, account_id)` key prevents duplicate history entries. Active membership keeps its current capabilities. Removed people can rejoin only through an explicit fresh invitation issued after removal. Reset prior capabilities on rejoin and use a membership generation that a restore cannot reuse. Retries and replay must respect the generation and journal ordering. Commit acceptance, membership, receipt and audit together. Test concurrent accept/accept, accept/cancel, accept/resend and accept/remove with deterministic assertions for both orderings. [Journey schema](../../packages/backend/src/modules/journeys/schema.ts)

**Restrict and recover.** A removal/leave transaction commits the local deny, policy generation, minimal event, audit and retry job. Extend the restriction journal’s independent append/receipt/replay mechanism with a versioned event and digest covering journey ID, member account ID and membership generation. Keep version 1 identity receipt verification and replay compatible. Apply each restriction to its specific journey and membership generation. Mark removal confirmed only after a verified durable receipt. On uncertain append, keep access closed and show **Access removed · confirmation pending** until the retry confirms it. Invalidate open detail and People on focus, reconnect, login and on a bounded foreground revalidation interval. Reconcile history and Dashboard through the same active-member reads. Withhold stale private shared views while foreground access cannot be verified. Resume from a restore with protected reads closed until journal replay. Invalidate restored outstanding invitations, their continuation handles and old mail jobs through the recovery epoch. Keep account-local private originals separate. [Restriction and restore rules](../core/architecture.md#154-backup-restore-and-release) · [Current journal format](../../packages/backend/src/modules/lifecycle/restriction-journal.ts)

## 5. Email delivery and Mailpit

- Extend the existing worker and SMTP transport. Use a separate journey invitation message and lifecycle with the same transactional pg-boss pattern as verification email. Write the encrypted render payload in the same transaction as the invitation and audit, and pass the worker only a row ID. Before dispatch recheck pending state, revision, expiry, inviter authority and recovery epoch. Already-dispatched mail can still arrive after cancellation. The link must fail when opened. Retry on transient SMTP failure with backoff and keep a stable message ID. A crash after SMTP accepts the send can produce a duplicate email with the same validity, so acceptance must remain idempotent. [Existing identity mail worker](../../packages/backend/src/modules/identity/email.ts) · [SMTP transport](../../packages/backend/src/mail.ts)
- Use a simple text and HTML message with the inviter’s display name, the role, **Review invitation**, and the seven-day expiry. Keep the private journey title and schedule on the verified review screen. No contact import or sharing links in this slice.
- Run development and acceptance tests against Mailpit. The default host SMTP endpoint is `127.0.0.1:51025` and the local inbox is `http://127.0.0.1:58025`. Keep recipient fixtures synthetic. Devices use a reachable development web origin for the link; a phone’s own loopback cannot reach the host web app. Exercise the signed-out, existing account and signup/verification flows via the inbox. Real SMTP delivery and verified production links require staging evidence before external use. [Development Compose configuration](../../infra/compose/dev.yml)

### 6.1 Shared service progress

The People and invitable-journey endpoints return up to 25 rows in stable UUID order. Each page checks the current session and journey authority. The roster returns account IDs, display names and capability lists. An empty capability list represents a Participant. The journey picker returns titles and the existing schedule projection, including a null schedule for eligible legacy journeys. [Roster migration](../../packages/backend/drizzle/0007_journey_people.sql) · [Shared contracts](../../packages/contracts/src/journeys.ts)

Administrators can issue up to 10 addresses per request. Limits are 30 sends per organiser, 20 per journey, three to one address per organiser, and 12 to one address across all journeys per hour. Sends to one address within a journey have a one-minute cooldown. Each organiser can record up to 120 new invitation operations per hour, with exact retries served from the recorded outcome. The worker clears encrypted render data after delivery, terminal failure or expiry. It removes addresses and token digests eligible for cleanup seven days after link expiry and clears batch retry records after 14 days.

The email contains a seven-day fragment credential. The exchange API returns an opaque continuation valid for up to 30 minutes. Verified review issues a continuation bound to the recipient account. Explicit acceptance opens the original journey and stores one receipt bound to the accepted membership generation. A repeat of the committing operation can recover its result. A new operation after acceptance returns `invitation_accepted`, and review can open the existing journey while membership is current. The browser landing route and account handoff are part of the end-to-end web slice.

The [isolated PostgreSQL and Mailpit suite](../../tests/integration/isolated-journeys.sh) passed 26 checks after independent review. Four invitees joined one canonical journey and appeared once each in history and Dashboard. Tests covered signup and verification, SMTP retry, queue rollback, replay after revocation and rejoin, both orderings of acceptance against cancellation and resend, mailbox quotas across journeys, and suppression of old links and mail after a recovery epoch rotation. The review also brought the invitation routines into readiness checks and normalized the display name in invitation email. ### 6.2 Membership removal and recovery

People includes the generation of each active membership. Remove and leave require that generation and a stable idempotency key. A stale generation produces a conflict and an exact retry returns the original operation. The last active administrator cannot leave. The local transaction revokes access, advances journey policy, cancels related pending invitations, and records the audit event and confirmation job. The API returns HTTP 202 while the deny awaits independent confirmation.

Version 2 journal events scope the deny to a journey, account and membership generation. Version 1 identity receipts keep their existing digest. A newer deny for the same journey and account waits for earlier denies to be confirmed before it can be appended. This covers backups from before a rejoin. Replaying an older deny preserves a later membership generation. The generation backfill for legacy memberships is reproducible when an older backup is migrated.

The [membership integration suite](../../tests/integration/journey-membership.test.ts) exercises mixed replay against simulated earlier database state, corrupt and unknown journal entries, and a lost append reply. The [invitation integration suite](../../tests/integration/journey-invitations.test.ts) forces both orderings of acceptance against removal and retries a removal after the recipient rejoins. ### 6.3 Browser People and invitations

People lists current members separately from pending invitations. Organisers can use **Find on Kairn** for members who enable invitation search in Account, or **Invite by email** for any recipient address. Search accepts a display-name prefix or an exact verified email. Results show display names and require an exact email when multiple members have the same name. Search is scoped to administrators of the selected journey, with bounded results and short-lived selections. The send transaction checks that the selected member is still discoverable and the verified address is current. Pending rows for a selected member display their name.

The browser removes the email credential from the address bar and keeps one continuation in the current tab. It carries the review destination through sign-in, verification and required setup. Review withholds private information during revalidation, and explicit acceptance opens the original journey. An unconfirmed batch stays available when the organiser closes and reopens the composer. A lost leave response can be reconciled with its original operation key after the journey becomes unavailable.

## Astra review and revisions

GPT-6.2 Astra confirmed the shared journey ID, Participant role, and explicit acceptance fit the requirements. It requested four revisions before implementation. The revised plan adds account- and membership-bound acceptance receipts, versioned restriction events with mixed-journal replay coverage, typed return paths with explicit account-switch and verification recovery, and precise organiser behavior for journey selection, partial batches and the optional checkpoint. It also records the last-administrator limit and the later transfer and freeze workflows.
